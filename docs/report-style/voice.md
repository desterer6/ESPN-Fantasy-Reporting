# Voice and format

The reports are a group-chat newsletter, not a sports column. Facts set up the story and the jokes
make it worth reading. The published example in [examples/](examples/) beats any rule here.

## Consistent vs. fresh

David wants every report to feel like the same newsletter while staying fun and fresh.

| Keep the same every week | Change every week |
|---|---|
| Section order, headers, dividers and title-line emoji signature for each league | Comparisons, punchlines and closing lines |
| Facts in every matchup: final score, both new records, streaks that matter | Tier names (never reused) |
| Manager nicknames and each team's storyline premise | The next chapter of each storyline |
| Option B math, tier splitting and how movement is measured | The current-events hook |
| ALL CAPS for the outrageous, normal case for supporting stats, emoji at sentence ends | Which stat leads, so the format doesn't read like a template |

## Structure of a weekly post

1. **Title line:** `# 🏈🔥 <LEAGUE> 2026 - WEEK <N> RECAP 🔥🏈` plus a trailing emoji string
   (Fayetteville's Finest uses `👅🏓🚬🦅🫦🫪`, Yellowstone uses `🤠🦅🚬🫪`).
2. **Opening paragraph, all caps** (FF opens "WELL LADIES AND GENTLEMEN", Yellowstone "WELL WELL WELL"): the state of the league in one breath. Hit the top teams, the
   biggest collapse, the worst team, and the playoff race, each with an absurd comparison.
3. **One `###` section per matchup**, titled `Team A vs. Team B` with the exact team names.
4. A `---------` divider, then **AI POWER RANKINGS** with tiers, then **POWER RANKINGS INTELLIGENCE REPORT**.
5. A `----------` divider, then **WEEK <N+1> MATCHUP TO WATCH**.
6. No wrap-up paragraph after the matchups.

## Each matchup section

- Lead with a **bold all-caps headline sentence** carrying the section's one comedic premise.
- Back it with two or three facts: final score, the standout or the disaster, and records after the week.
- Always state the final score (`156.78-86.96`), both new records, and any streak worth noting.
- End on an all-caps escalation of the premise, ending in a string of emoji.
- Mix in one or two normal-case sentences for the supporting stat lines, as in the example.
- Keep it short. One premise per matchup, and drop any stat the joke doesn't need.

## Tone rules learned from David's edits

- Use aggressive, ridiculous comparisons (lottery without a ticket, Super PAC donations,
  Geneva Convention, Titanic customer service) instead of generic sports commentary.
- Keep it fresh and current (David's stated goal). Running jokes evolve week to week, and no
  comparison is reused for at least 4 weeks. See [used-references.md](used-references.md).
- Intentional nonsense and informal phrasing are welcome ("BROTHER HAS NOW WON", "HOW IS THIS LEGAL⁉").
- Use ALL CAPS for the outrageous developments, normal case for supporting numbers. Not every sentence is caps.
- Refer to managers by first name or nickname (Gage, Tucker, Odom, CGreer/Greer, Silas, Repp).
- Use emoji heavily, in clusters at sentence ends. Favorites: 😭💀🚬🫪🥀🤮☢🦅📉📈🚨🫵.
- Keep it a roast between friends, never mean about anything outside fantasy football.

## Power rankings format

```
🏈🔥 <LEAGUE> — WEEK <N> AI POWER RANKINGS 🔥🏈<emoji string>
<emoji><emoji> <NEW ABSURD TIER NAME>

* Team name (+2)
* Team name
```

Movement is `(+N)` or `(-N)` against last week's published ranking. Unchanged teams get no marker.
Tier names change every week. The math decides the groups, and the comedy decides the names.
Close with one all-caps **INTELLIGENCE REPORT** paragraph explaining the biggest moves.

## Matchup to watch format

`👀🔥 WEEK <N> MATCHUP TO WATCH 🔥👀`, then `Team A (W-L) vs. Team B (W-L)`, then one all-caps
paragraph. It frames the fight, gives recent scores for both, quotes the platform's projection,
spells out what each result does to the records, and ends with an absurd escalation.
